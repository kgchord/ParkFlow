# ParkFlow: Technical Challenges, Risks and Mitigation

Every number here is either taken from a cited datasheet or standard, or worked out from the design geometry and labelled **Predicted**. Nothing is a measured result yet. The month numbers refer to `docs/roadmap/ROADMAP.md` (M1 = Nov 2026 … M4 = Feb 2027).

---

## R1. Night-time plate glare and IR overexposure

**Predicted Risk:** At night the 850 nm IR illuminator and headlamps make the retro-reflective plate "bloom", so characters merge and OCR fails. Motion blur adds to this. Example: a car at 15 km/h (4.2 m/s) seen by a 1920 px sensor across a 3 m field (≈1.6 mm/px) blurs by **≈11 px at 1/250 s** but only **≈2.7 px at 1/1000 s**. Default "auto" exposure at night tends to choose the slow shutter.
**Mitigation:** Fixed fast shutter (≤1/1000 s) with an IR-only night profile. WDR on, IR power reduced until the plate is not saturated. Camera tilted 15 to 25° down so headlamps are not on-axis. A second "plate exposure" frame is taken on each trigger. An ANPR confidence below threshold falls back to RFID + guard check; the system never hard-blocks a vehicle.
**Verification:** Plate-level OCR accuracy and the share of saturated plate pixels on recorded night video (to be verified in Month 2); live night passes at the pilot gate, part of AC2 ≥95 % fused auto-pass (to be verified in Month 4).

## R2. RFID reading a tag in the adjacent lane

**Predicted Risk:** The reader is specified for a **≥6 m** read range, and WPC rules allow up to **2 W e.r.p.** (G.S.R. 853(E), 2021). A typical lane is about 3 m wide, so a tag in the next lane is **inside the read range**. Without mitigation, the wrong lane may open or the wrong vehicle may be billed (FMEA #2, RPN 120, joint highest with #10).
**Mitigation:** Circular-polarised antenna tilted down at the 3 to 5 m read zone. Conducted power stepped down to the lowest level that still gives reliable reads in this lane. RSSI threshold plus "peak-RSSI" time-stamp. **Fusion gate:** an EPC is used only if this lane's camera reads the matching plate within ±500 ms. RF absorber or metal mesh on the lane-divider side.
**Verification:** Two-lane test with a tagged car parked at 3 m lateral offset, 100 passes. Target: **0 cross-lane decisions** after mitigation; raw cross-reads logged before and after (to be verified in Month 3).

## R3. Edge Node thermal throttling in a sealed IP65 box at 45 °C

**Predicted Risk:** The Raspberry Pi 5 starts throttling at **80 °C** SoC (85 °C hard limit) [H1], and the AI HAT+ is rated for **0 to 50 °C ambient** [H2]; the Netgear GS305P is rated 0 to 40 °C. `cad/calcs/CALCS.md` §5 (re-run 25 Oct) estimates **28.7 W** inside a box that holds everything (Pi 5 + Hailo-8 12 W, supply loss 6.6 W, PoE switch 4 W, the rest 6.1 W), natural convection h = 5 W/m²K. For the **bought 300×300×210 mm box (0.342 m²)** the internal air at 45 °C ambient is **61.8 °C sealed** and **54.2 °C with a 200×150 mm through-wall sink**, both above 50 °C (the CAD box 300×400×180 gives 60.4 and 53.5 °C: box size changes the result by under 1 K). Moving the 24 V supply and mains devices to a separate small power box removes 6.6 W and gives **52.3 °C on convection alone, 48.1 °C counting radiation**: this straddles the limit, so the margin is **not demonstrated**. The highest ambient that keeps the air at 50 °C is 42.7 °C (46.9 °C with radiation). Unshaded sun adds about 32 W, so a sun hood is mandatory. Predicted consequence: throttled ANPR → latency above 300 ms; the GS305P is outside its rating in every case. (An earlier draft of this section quoted 15 to 25 W and 65 °C from a rougher estimate; the CALCS figures replace it.)
**Mitigation:** Sun hood (2 mm Al, 25 mm air gap) and a light-grey enclosure. Aluminium heat spreader from the SoC/Hailo to the wall, the through-wall sink, and the **24 V supply in a separate shaded power box** (decided 25 Oct). An ePTFE vent only equalises pressure. A fan that vents the box was rejected in CALCS because it drops the box to IP54. Software lowers the frame rate at 75 °C; the Pi 5 Active Cooler stirs the internal air. 70/80 °C bimetal cut-offs (SAFETY F7). If the soak shows throttling: larger sink, lower ANPR frame rate, an industrial PoE switch (OPEN_DECISIONS D8), or the Jetson Orin Nano Super in the same enclosure (its thermal load has not been analysed).
**Verification:** 4 h soak at 45 °C with ANPR at full rate; `vcgencmd get_throttled` must stay 0x0 (AC4). Internal air, SoC, Hailo and switch temperatures are logged and compared with the 50 °C and 40 °C ratings; the prediction says this will be marginal for the HAT+ and failing for the GS305P (to be verified in Month 3, repeated on site in Month 4). The heat sink, sun hood, vent and thermostats are estimate lines in the BOM (no vendor page found).

## R4. Non-standard or decorative Indian plates and two-wheelers

**Predicted Risk:** OCR models trained on standard plates fail on decorative fonts, two-row plates, stickers or bent two-wheeler plates. Two-wheelers also usually **carry no FASTag** (FASTag is aimed at four-wheelers on NETC tolls: general knowledge, not cited; the pilot measures the real share), so fusion falls back to ANPR alone for them. The share of non-standard plates on campus is **unknown** and will be measured, not assumed.
**Mitigation:** Separate two-row and single-row OCR heads. Indian state-code and series regex with a check against the access list (edit distance ≤1 → guard soft-confirm). Fine-tune on the team's recorded campus video. Two-wheelers: plate + app QR or a resident pass. Per-plate-type accuracy reported separately, so an average does not hide a weak class.
**Verification:** Breakdown of plate types and per-class OCR accuracy on recorded video (to be verified in Month 2); two-wheeler pass rate at the pilot (to be verified in Month 4).

### Watch-list (tracked in FMEA)

- **Barrier torque vs boom length** (predicted, from `cad/calcs/CALCS.md` §4, re-run 25 Oct): the 1.2 m 60×30×1.5 Al boom is about 1.0 kg with fittings → 6.9 N·m gravity moment about the shaft; the 7-plate counterweight (3.30 kg, 94 % balance) leaves 0.4 N·m of closing torque (0.34 N at the tip), so the boom can only close if unpowered. At a 15 m/s operating wind the required drive torque is **11.6 N·m** (30 mm face with a worst-oblique 48.5 mm equivalent; 13.8 N·m with the old 60 mm face). The BOM motor is listed at 18 N·m, margin 1.56 at 15 m/s, so it is a **demo motor limited to wind ≤ 12 m/s** (margin 2.2); the ≥ 30 N·m self-locking production motor is still open (OPEN_DECISIONS D1). Self-locking is not assumed. **Raised boom in the 47 m/s design gust (previously unanalysed):** 93 N·m back-driving moment (57 N·m on the 30 mm face, 115 N·m on the 60 mm bounding face; the earlier "46 N·m" used Cf 1.2 and a smaller boom). The gearbox cannot be assumed to hold it, so a storm-lock pin carries the load and the guard lowers and pins the boom on a storm warning (no anemometer in the BOM). Closed boom in the same gust: the breakaway pin is at 89 % of its break moment. Check in Month 1 FEA and the Month 3 static hold test.
- **NETC acquirer dependency**: real FASTag debits need an NPCI-certified acquirer bank, so Stage 2/3 uses a **mock NETC ledger** that mirrors the acquirer flow. Live demo = real EPC/TID read + mock debit. Any acquirer partnership is a post-Stage-3 item and is not on the critical path.
