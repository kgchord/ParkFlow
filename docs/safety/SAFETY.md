# ParkFlow Demo Lane: Safety, FMEA and Standards

Scope: the Stage 2 prototype "ParkFlow Demo Lane" (one lane gantry pole, IP65 Edge Node enclosure, Lane Controller PCB, UHF RFID reader, ANPR camera, 1.2 m demo boom barrier, 4 occupancy nodes, P10 LED sign).
Status: design-stage analysis, 24 Oct 2026; reviewed and updated 25 Oct 2026 (contactor, supply, mains-loss behaviour, thermal; see `docs/OPEN_DECISIONS.md`). Every threshold below is a **design target, to be bench-verified** (Months 2 to 4, see `docs/roadmap/ROADMAP.md`). No test results are claimed. Where the current BOM does not yet contain a part that a fail-safe needs, the row says so under **BOM status**.

## 1. Physical fail-safes

Rule: anything that can hurt a person (barrier motion, mains) is stopped by **hardware**, and software only adds to that. Firmware cannot override the E-stop, the fuses or the shear pin.

| # | Fail-safe | Implementation | What it protects against |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Hard-wired E-stop** | Red mushroom latching NC switch on the gantry pole, wired **hard-wired in series with the 24 V coil of the motor-power contactor K1 (Schneider LC1D09BD)**, so the motor supply (K1 contacts -> IBT-2 module B+/B-) is opened upstream of the H-bridge; it is not read through a GPIO. The same loop is sensed (ESTOP_OK) and forces the IBT-2 enables and the OPEN PWM low through the 74AHCT1G08 gates U9/U19, as a second path. The ProMax E-stop in the BOM has contacts rated 1 A DC (product page), so it cannot break motor current itself; the contactor does. **BOM status (25 Oct):** the hobby relay module has been replaced by the LC1D09BD in the BOM (₹1,226.02, Prem Associates, New Delhi); its coil power and DC contact rating are not on the listing and must be confirmed on the Schneider datasheet (assumed 0.2 A coil, three poles in series in the +24 V line). Force-guided (mirror-contact) auxiliary monitoring is not fitted. A second NC contact goes to an ESP32-S3 input so the event is logged. Reset needs a manual twist-release and then a "re-arm" press on the controller. | Any barrier hazard; works with the firmware hung or the PCB faulty. |
| F2 | **Anti-crush: photo-beam** | IR through-beam pair at 300 mm above grade across the boom path. Beam broken → closing is inhibited, and if the boom is already moving down it reverses to 90°. Beam is "dark-on-fault": a cut cable reads as "obstructed". The BOM receiver (Delta PS-MT5 series, PNP output) drives 24 V onto the active-high input while the beam is clear (light-ON mode, to be confirmed on the part), so a cut wire or lost supply reads as blocked. | Closing on a car bonnet, a two-wheeler or a pedestrian. |
| F3 | **Anti-crush: motor current-limit auto-reverse** | The IBT-2 module's current-sense outputs (IS pins, about 0.1 V/A, to be bench-calibrated) go to an ESP32-S3 ADC. Firmware trips at **1.5× the running current measured at commissioning** (target; the config placeholder of 1500 mV is about 14 A and must be set to about 540 mV for the 5 A limit), held 50 ms → stop, then reverse to open. **Correction 25 Oct:** earlier text described a low-side shunt and a hardware comparator that also gates the enable; the schematic has no such comparator, so the trip is firmware-only (it needs the firmware running; the E-stop path F1 does not). A hardware comparator is an optional addition (OPEN_DECISIONS D7). The 5 A limit also protects the 24 V supply (peak 7.66 A of 8.8 A). | Contact the beam does not see (object above 300 mm, beam misaligned). |
| F4 | **Presence sensor close-inhibit** | Presence sensor in the lane under the boom. The boom does not start to close while the sensor is occupied. **BOM status (25 Oct):** an inductive loop detector (PD132, 230 V AC, relay output; Golf Automation, Noida, ₹2,124) is now in the BOM; its relay is wired between +24V_FIELD and the PRESENCE input. Relay rating and loop installation are to be confirmed. | Vehicle stopped under the boom. |
| F5 | **Breakaway shear-pin boom** | Boom attached to the hub by a sacrificial shear pin (5 mm 6061-T6 pin; calculated to break at about 120 N·m hinge moment, i.e. 104 N at the tip; the 47 m/s design gust on the closed boom loads it to 89 % of that, so a nuisance break is possible in the strongest gust and spare pins are in the BOM; sized so that it fails below the gearbox output-shaft rating, to be FEA-checked). Boom held captive by a lanyard. | A vehicle driving through a closed boom: the boom swings away and the gearbox, motor and pole are not damaged. Lowers the energy that reaches a person or vehicle. |
| F6 | **Fusing, MCB, RCCB, SPD, TVS** | 230 VAC input: 2-pole MCB (C-curve) + 30 mA RCCB + Type 2 SPD on DIN rail. DC: separate fuses on the 24 V motor rail, the 24 V controller rail, the PoE/12 V rail and the 5 V/5 A Pi rail. **BOM status (25 Oct):** the BOM has two 1-pole 6 A MCBs, 5×20 mm glass fuse holders (not blade fuses), a Type 2 SPD and now a Schneider 2-pole 25 A 30 mA RCCB (₹1,062, Trademinds, Mumbai); the MCB/RCCB/SPD/supplies sit in a separate IP65 power box on the pole base. The MCBs are 1P while this row says 2-pole: confirm which conductor is switched. TVS diodes on RS485, photo-beam, limit-switch and E-stop sense lines (these are field cables). Reverse-polarity protection on the PCB 24 V input. | Short circuits, shock, lightning and switching surges on outdoor cable runs, and one fault taking down all rails. |
| F7 | **Thermal cut-off in the Edge Node enclosure** | Three stages: (a) passive: sun hood, light-coloured box, heat spreader and a through-wall finned sink, and the 24 V supply moved to a separate power box (`cad/calcs/CALCS.md` §5); a venting fan was rejected there because it lowers the box to IP54. **Predicted internal air at 45 °C ambient (bought 300×300×210 box): sealed 61.8 °C; with the sink 54.2 °C; with the supply also moved out 52.3 °C on convection alone (48.1 °C counting radiation), against the 50 °C AI HAT+ rating and the 40 °C GS305P rating. This stage is therefore NOT shown to be sufficient**; the highest ambient that keeps the air at 50 °C is 42.7 °C (46.9 °C with radiation); (b) software: SoC above 75 °C → ANPR frame rate is lowered and an alert raised, before the Pi 5's own 80 °C throttle point [H1]; this derating is the main defence if the air exceeds 50 °C; (c) hardware: NC bimetal thermostat (KSD301 type, 70 °C) on a GPIO for a graceful shutdown, plus a second NC bimetal (80 °C) in series with the Edge Node DC-DC input for a hard cut. **BOM status:** the thermostats, heat sink, sun hood and vent are estimate lines (no vendor page found). The Lane Controller is on its own rail and treats the lost heartbeat as F9. | Fire or component damage in a sealed box; silent wrong decisions from a throttled node. |
| F8 | **IP65 sealing** | Wall enclosure rated IP65 (IEC 60529: dust-tight, protected against water jets) [S3]. All cables enter through IP-rated cable glands; drip loops; the vent is an IP-rated breather. Outdoor RFID reader, camera and P10 module must each carry a maker's IP rating: the CP Plus camera listing says IP67, the ID Tech reader says IP65 on TradeIndia but no rating on IndustryBuying, and the Probots P10 listing gives no rating (the Navi Mumbai alternative says IP67). Confirm each in writing before ordering. **BOM status (25 Oct):** the heat sink, sun hood, ePTFE vent and M25/M20/M16 glands are carried as estimate ranges (no vendor page found), not vendor prices. | Monsoon rain, dust, condensation. |
| F9 | **Watchdog and safe-state on loss of brain** | The ESP32-S3 hardware watchdog resets the controller. The Edge Node sends a heartbeat to the controller every 1 s; if 3 s pass with no heartbeat, the controller goes to **open-and-hold** mode, sets the LED amber and sounds the buzzer for the guard. | Edge Node hang, OS crash, thermal shutdown. |
| F10 | **Earthing and bonding** | Gantry pole, base plate, barrier housing and enclosure back-plate are bonded with a green/yellow PE conductor to the site earth (IS 3043:2018 practice [S7]). Earth continuity and loop values are measured and recorded at installation. The SMPS is Class I. | Shock from a frame that has become live; it also gives the SPD a path to earth. |
| F11 | **Mains-loss behaviour: fail-in-place with a hand release (baseline, decided 25 Oct)** | The earlier claim ("drive the boom open on stored energy; a self-locking worm holds it") is **withdrawn**: no 24 V backup is in the BOM and the listed wiper motor does not state that it is self-locking. Baseline behaviour: on mains loss the 24 V bus, the Lane Controller and the motor drive are unpowered and the boom stays where it is, or, because the counterweight leaves it boom-heavy (94 % balance), it can only close slowly with a tip force of 0.34 N (CALCS §4), which cannot crush. A guard releases the boom **by hand** with the lanyarded quick-release pin (BOM: storm-lock/manual-release pin, estimate) and swings the 1.0 kg boom aside. The Pi (X1202 UPS, target ≥ 10 min, to be bench-verified) and the RFID reader (12 V mini UPS) keep logging; the PoE switch and camera have **no UPS**, so ANPR is lost during an outage and the lane falls back to RFID plus guard. The firmware's AC_OK open-hold path stays in the code and activates only if a 24 V buffer is fitted (J6 AC-OK contact). **Optional upgrade (not in the BOM or the total):** a 24 V battery buffer and a DC-UPS module, or a self-locking ≥ 30 N·m gearmotor, would restore automatic fail-open (OPEN_DECISIONS D2, D1). | People trapped during a power cut or an evacuation: mitigated by a 1 kg hand-movable boom and a guard procedure, not by automation. |
| F12 | **Mechanical end-stops + limit switches** | Hard stops at 0° and 90° behind the limit flags. A limit switch that is missed is caught by a motion time-out (target 1.5× the nominal travel time), which stops the motor. | Over-travel, stalled motor (see FMEA row 8). |

### Why fail-open is still the intent (F11)

*Status 25 Oct: this is the design intent and the site-policy default; the prototype baseline does not implement automatic fail-open on mains loss (see the F11 row). The reasons below are why the upgrade options in OPEN_DECISIONS D1/D2 matter.*

1. **Life safety beats revenue.** A parking exit is an evacuation route. A boom stuck closed during a power cut, a fire or an earthquake traps vehicles. A boom stuck open only loses some revenue or control for a short time.
2. **Fits the Stage 1 promise.** Stage 1 says the system "never hard blocks a vehicle without a human reviewable alternative". Failing open, with a guard alert, keeps that promise.
3. **Revenue can still be recovered.** The Edge Node keeps logging plate and tag events on UPS power and syncs them later, so open-lane passages are billed or reconciled afterwards (mock NETC ledger in the prototype).
4. **Sites can choose fail-secure.** A high-security site can set fail-secure in configuration, but this requires a manned guard post and a manual-release key at the lane. The default is fail-open.

## 2. Design FMEA (10 rows)

Scales are 1 to 10. **S** = severity, **O** = occurrence, **D** = detection (1 = almost certain to be detected before harm, 10 = not detectable). RPN = S × O × D. Scores are **design-stage team estimates**; they will be re-scored after the Month 3 to 4 tests.

| # | Failure mode | Effect / hazard | S | O | D | RPN | Embedded fail-safe / mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ANPR plate misread** (glare, dirt, non-standard font, two-wheeler) | Fusion mismatch → a verified user is held up; at worst, the wrong account is billed | 5 | 6 | 3 | 90 | Two-signal rule: RFID EPC and the plate must both match the access list. Confidence threshold, Indian plate-format regex and state-code check. Edit distance of 1 or less, with the tag matching, gives a "soft match" sent to the guard. Below threshold → QR check or guard fallback (Stage 1 §3.1). |
| 2 | **RFID cross-lane read** (tag in the adjacent lane read) | Wrong lane opens, or the wrong vehicle is billed | 6 | 5 | 4 | **120** | Antenna tilted down at the read zone. Reader power stepped down to the lowest that gives reliable reads. RSSI gate and phase/time-window gate. Tag must match the plate seen by **this** lane's camera within ±500 ms. RF absorber on the lane-divider side (see RISKS R2). |
| 3 | **Cloned tag** (EPC copied; tag and plate disagree) | Fraudulent entry or billing to someone else | 7 | 3 | 3 | 63 | No auto-open when the plate and the EPC disagree. Gen2 TID (factory-programmed chip ID) is checked against the TID registered for that EPC. Guard alert with the plate crop. Event logged for later investigation. |
| 4 | **Edge Node hang** (OS, inference or driver crash) | No decisions; lane stalls | 6 | 3 | 2 | 36 | systemd watchdog on the Pi. Lane Controller heartbeat time-out of 3 s → open-and-hold, amber LED, buzzer (F9). Auto-reboot. Event queue survives in a local SQLite WAL. |
| 5 | **Network / internet loss** | Cloud sync and ledger posting stop | 3 | 6 | 2 | 36 | Offline-first: access list is cached on the node and entries/exits are queued locally, then replayed to the (mock) NETC ledger when the link returns (Stage 1 §3.3). Dashboard shows "offline since hh:mm". |
| 6 | **Mains loss** | Barrier stays (or slowly closes at 0.34 N tip force) with no motor power; a vehicle may be held until a guard hand-releases the boom; ANPR down (no UPS on PoE switch and camera) | 7 | 4 | 4 | 112 | Baseline (F11): fail-in-place + hand release with a lanyarded pin, 1.0 kg boom, 94 % counterweight so it cannot be driven open or closed hard; Pi UPS (target ≥ 10 min) and 12 V mini UPS keep logging and RFID. Detection score raised from 2 to 4 because the controller loses power too. RPN rises 56 → 112 until a 24 V buffer is fitted (OPEN_DECISIONS D2). |
| 7 | **Barrier closing on a vehicle or person** | Injury or vehicle damage | 10 | 3 | 3 | 90 | Photo-beam (F2) + presence inhibit (F4) + current-limit reverse (F3) + slow closing speed + shear-pin boom (F5) + hard-wired E-stop (F1). Light Al boom (≈1.0 kg with fittings, predicted in CALCS §4) to keep impact energy low; with the motor unpowered the counterweighted boom closes with only 0.34 N at the tip. |
| 8 | **Motor stall / limit switch missed** | Motor burnout, H-bridge overheats, boom half-way | 6 | 3 | 2 | 36 | Firmware current-limit trip (F3, 5 A), motion time-out (F12), fuse on the 24 V motor rail (F6), IBT-2 (BTS7960) thermal shutdown, E-stop contactor (F1). Fault latched until a technician resets it. |
| 9 | **Edge Node enclosure over-temperature** (45 °C ambient, sealed box, sun) | Throttled ANPR → late decisions; component ageing; shutdown | 6 | 5 | 3 | 90 | F7 three-stage thermal scheme, heat spreader on the Pi 5 SoC/AI HAT+, sun shade, light-coloured enclosure, 24 V supply moved to a separate power box, ePTFE breather (pressure equalisation only), temperature logged every 10 s, 75 °C frame-rate derating. Predicted air 52.3 °C (convection only) is above the 50 °C HAT+ rating, so O stays at 5 (see RISKS R3). |
| 10 | **Plate-data breach** (stolen node, intercepted sync, misused dashboard) | Personal data exposed; liability under DPDP Act 2023 | 8 | 3 | 5 | **120** | Raw video and plate crops are processed in RAM and thrown away. Plates are stored only as a salted HMAC token. LUKS full-disk encryption on the node. TLS for sync. Role-based dashboard access with audit log. Retention policy. Breach-response runbook that follows DPDP Rules 2025 r.7 (see §3). |

Highest RPN (120): **#2 RFID cross-lane read** and **#10 plate-data breach**. These two are tested first in Month 3 (RFID) and reviewed in a design review in Month 2 (data). The highest severity (10) is **#7**; it has five independent protective functions (F1 to F5) plus a slow closing speed, and each is to be tested in Month 3. Note: the presence sensor for F4 is now a loop detector in the BOM (relay rating to confirm).

## 3. Standards and regulatory compliance

Each standard's number, title and current edition was checked online on 24 Oct 2026 and re-checked on 25 Oct 2026 (sources in brackets; full list in `docs/REFERENCES.md`). IS 875-3:2015 and IS 732:2019 were confirmed only from secondary summaries, not from the BIS portal. EN standards are **not mandatory in India** for parking barriers. We use them as **reference test methods**, and we do not claim certification.

| Standard | Title / edition | How the design addresses it |
|---|---|---|
| **IS 875 (Part 3):2015** [S1] | Design loads (other than earthquake) for buildings and structures, Part 3: Wind loads (2015 revision) | Basic wind speed Vb = 47 m/s (Delhi listing, Zone IV) used for the NCR campus pilot. Confirm the site on Fig. 1; Chennai is 50 m/s and Bengaluru 33 m/s. With k1 = k2 = k3 = k4 = 1: Vz = 47 m/s, pz = 0.6·Vz² ≈ 1.33 kPa. This is the design load for the gantry pole, camera arm, RFID antenna, P10 sign and closed boom in the FEA recipes (not yet run; hand-calc predictions in `cad/calcs/CALCS.md`: pole FoS 2.15, base plate 2.89 at Delhi wind). A 40x40 pole is predicted to fail (FoS 0.46), so the pole is 80x80. Base-plate M12 anchors are checked for overturning moment (predicted tension 7.39 kN per bolt). Chennai on 6063-T5 is predicted at FoS 1.91, so use T6 there. |
